Basics 01

A implies B does not mean B implies A

People say one sentence, and then they use the reverse sentence as if it were the same fact. The first one can be fine. The second one is a different claim.

Videep Ekbote / All basics

A implies B

If it rains, the ground gets wet.

does not mean

B implies A

The ground is wet, so it rained.

The mix up

If A implies B, that only travels one way. A happened, so B follows. It does not tell you that B happened, so A must have happened. I keep hearing people treat those as the same sentence, including people who are very well trained. Doctors do it. Lawyers do it. Engineers, journalists and managers do it. So does everyone else.

There is a name for the habit. Logicians call the reversed sentence the converse, and they call the jump from B back to A affirming the consequent. I am mentioning the names so the habit has a label if you want to look it up. The useful part is hearing it in ordinary talk.

If it rains, the ground gets wet. The ground is wet, so it rained.

The first half is usually fine. The second half does not follow. Someone may have washed a car. A pipe may be leaking. The gardener may have watered the plants. The dew may have been heavy. Wet ground is the result. Rain is only one of the things that can produce it.

The key thing is the direction. A rule gets stated one way, and a few minutes later the same person uses it backwards and does not notice. Once you can hear the weather pair as two different sentences, you start hearing the same pair everywhere.

Relationships

If he cared, he would remember the birthday. He remembered, so he cares.

Remembering a date can be a phone reminder, a friend who sent a message in the morning, guilt, habit, or a person who is good with dates and careless with everything else. The memory is real. The feeling is a guess. People still treat the guess as if the memory had proved it.

If someone is cheating, they pull away. She pulled away, so she is cheating. Pulling away has a long list behind it. A bad month at work. A parent who is unwell. A fight they have not found the words for. Low mood. A phone that keeps dying. A person who goes quiet when they are tired and nothing is wrong. The cheating story is the one people are afraid of, so it gets promoted from one possibility to the explanation. I think this is one of the most painful versions of the flip, because the fear feels like evidence.

If someone is interested, they make a plan. They made a plan, so they are interested. People make plans out of politeness, loneliness, boredom, and the fact that saying yes to Saturday is easier than saying no. A plan shows that a plan was made. It does not measure interest. (There is a cousin of this mistake, where no plan is treated as proof of no interest. That one flips a different part of the sentence. This piece is about taking the result and declaring the cause.)

If a relationship is healthy, the two people do not fight. You have not fought in months, so the relationship is healthy. Quiet can be peace. Quiet can also be two people who stopped bringing up the topics that cause fights. No fighting is the result you can see. Health is only one possible cause.

If they were serious about you, they would introduce you to their friends. They introduced you, so they are serious. Introductions also happen because a dinner was already on, because it would have been rude not to, because they like showing people their city. One evening with their friends is B. Seriousness was only one possible A.

Health

If you have the flu, you get a fever. You have a fever, so you have the flu.

A fever is a result with many causes. Some other infection. A reaction to something you took. Heat. A medicine. In a house, the sentence that actually gets used is the second one. You are warm, so it is the flu, take this. The first sentence never earned that.

If this tablet works, the pain goes down. The pain went down, so the tablet worked. Pain also goes down because you lay still, because an hour passed, because you slept, because pain does that on its own. The tablet may have done it. The clock does not tell you. This is how a home remedy becomes the family cure. The headache left after the ginger tea, so the ginger tea fixed it. The headache may already have been on its way out. Remedies can help. The order of events is still a different claim from a cause.

Doctors do this too. If it is bacterial, a marker they ordered tends to be high. The marker comes back high, so it is bacterial, so here are antibiotics. Other things raise the same marker. A careful doctor holds the sentence in the original direction and treats the number as a reason to look harder. A rushed clinic, or a frightened patient who wants a name for the feeling, flips it. The degree on the wall does not delete the flip. It gives the flip a printout.

If you eat junk every day, you gain weight. They gained weight, so they must be eating junk. Weight moves with sleep, medicine, muscle, age, a thyroid, a year of stress, a body doing something you cannot see from across a table. The junk food story is available, and it has a bit of moral tone, so it gets used. You can watch very educated people do this about a colleague in the time it takes to walk from the lift to the desk.

If a person is fit, they can get through a short run. They got through the run, so they are fit. One afternoon of running is the result. Someone can drag themselves through it and still be sleeping four hours and living on instant noodles. The run happened. The rest of the health claim was added for free.

If the treatment is working, the cough gets quieter. The cough got quieter, so the treatment is working. Coughs quiet down because the air changed, because they skipped cigarettes for three days, because a virus ran its course. Parents like the second sentence. So do clinicians who are in a hurry and would like the decision they already made to be the right one.

Money

If a person is rich, they have a nice car. They have a nice car, so they are rich.

Cars are leased, borrowed, gifted, and bought on a loan that will hurt for years. The car is in the parking lot. The loan is not. People still do this math out loud at weddings and outside offices. A chartered accountant will do it about a neighbour and then go back to a spreadsheet. The training and the flip live in the same person.

If you handle money well, you have some left at the end of the month. You have some left, so you handle money well. You may have moved back in with your parents. You may have got a bonus. You may have skipped a bill you still owe. Money left over is the result. Skill is one cause. Looking comfortable is a weaker version of the same flip. The holiday photos are up, so the decisions must be good.

If a business is failing, the stock price falls. The price fell, so the business is failing. Prices fall because rates moved, because the whole sector had a bad week, because one headline was ugly, because a large holder sold. A falling price is not a report on the health of the company. People who work with numbers all day still talk as if the chart closed the case. Engineers who trade do it. Managers do it at lunch. The numbers do not protect them from the direction change.

If a scheme is a scam, the promised return looks absurd. The return looks absurd, so it is a scam. Sometimes that absurd number really is a scam. Sometimes a young business, a one time offer, or a number quoted without the risk next to it also looks absurd. "It looks too good" is a different sentence from "this is a fraud". You still have to ask what else produces a wild number. And you still should not hand your savings to a stranger just because the flip might be wrong. A gap in the proof is not a reason to feel safe.

If they were careless with money, the account would be empty. The account is empty, so they were careless. Empty also comes from a medical bill, a late salary, money sent home, a theft, a year when the rent jumped. The empty account is the result. Calling it a character flaw is the flip, and relatives are very confident about it.

Careers

If someone is good at the job, they get promoted. They got promoted, so they are good.

Promotions follow timing, a boss who likes you, being in the room when a slot opens, a team that happened to do well, and sometimes actual skill. People treat the new title as a clean measurement of the person. It is a measurement of who received the title.

If you work hard, you stay late. They stay late, so they work hard. Staying late is also slow work, bad planning, a long commute they are avoiding, or a person who wants the chair to look occupied. People use this as management. The chair is what the floor can see. The work is not sitting in the chair. Managers with MBAs say it with a straight face. So do people who have never sat in a management class. The degree does not stop the sentence.

If a candidate is strong, they went to a well known college. They went to that college, so they are strong. This one lives in rooms full of engineers and hiring managers, including people who will tell you in the same meeting that they know better. The college can be one exam on one day, a coaching centre, a city, money at home. It can also be years of being very good. The certificate does not say which. Hiring still uses the flip because it is fast, and because everyone else in the room is nodding.

If the code is wrong, the tests fail. The tests failed, so the code is wrong. The test can be wrong. The data can be wrong. The setup can be wrong. Red on the screen feels like engineering because a machine printed it. It is still the flipped sentence. Anyone who has lost an hour to a bad test already knows the test can be the broken thing.

If a student is serious, the grades are high. The grades are high, so the student is serious. An easy paper, a lucky set, help they are not mentioning, or a term where the hard course was dropped, all produce the same mark. Faculty do this about students. Students do it about each other. High marks are the result. Seriousness is one route to them.

If a founder is serious, they have raised money. They raised money, so they are serious. Money shows up for a clean story, a known school, a hot market, a friend of an investor. Seriousness is one input. A wire transfer is the result, not a personality test.

News

If a story matters, it leads the page. It leads the page, so it matters.

The top of a page follows fear, clicks, and whatever happened in the last hour. A fire nearby can outrank a slow problem that will hit more people. Journalists know this about their own desk and still feel the pull when they are the ones scrolling. Placement is the result. Importance is only one reason a desk chose it.

If a source is lying, the account has holes. The account has holes, so the source is lying. Holes come from memory, fear, a bad recording, a person who was not looking at the thing you asked about. A careful reporter treats a hole as a reason to ask again. The flipped version treats the hole as a verdict, and that verdict gets published by people whose job is to check things. The job does not make the flip impossible. It makes the flip sound like a finding.

If a video is fake, it looks a bit off. It looks a bit off, so it is fake. Real footage looks off all the time. Bad light, a cheap phone, compression, a strange angle. "It looks weird" is a different sentence from "someone made this up". The last few years made the second sentence very cheap to say, including for people who were trained to verify footage before they post it.

If a report is solid, a big outlet ran it. A big outlet ran it, so it is solid. Big outlets also run things that are thin, early, or copied from each other on a deadline. The logo is the result of a decision made in a hurry. It is not a second investigation that you watched happen.

Parenting

If a child is happy at school, they talk about it at dinner. They talked about school, so they are happy.

They may have talked because you asked, because one funny thing happened, because they are a child who talks. A story about lunch is not a report on the whole day. Parents want it to be, because the alternative is not knowing, and not knowing is uncomfortable.

If a parent is neglectful, the child acts out. The child is acting out, so the parent is neglectful. Acting out also comes from a new sibling, a bully, hunger, a class they cannot follow, a week of bad sleep. Relatives and teachers, including very qualified teachers, jump to the parent because that was the cause they already had in mind. The behaviour is the result. The home is one place to look, not a closed case.

If you are raising them well, they are polite to guests. They were polite, so you are raising them well. One evening of greeting people can be fear, a promised treat, or a child who is good with guests and miserable at home. The guests still announce the verdict in the car. They saw B. They declared A.

If the child studied, the marks came out well. The marks came out well, so the child studied. The paper may have been kind. They may have guessed well. They may have been drilled on that exact paper. Good marks are the result you can put on the fridge. Studying is one cause, and it is the cause adults prefer, so it gets treated as the only one.

Workplace

If someone has checked out, they keep the camera off. The camera is off, so they have checked out.

The camera is also off for a weak connection, a sleeping baby, a face they do not want in a recording, or a person who is actually typing. Managers like this flip because the camera is the only evidence the meeting gives them. A person can run a large team and still use a black rectangle as a personality test.

If a meeting was useful, people spoke. People spoke, so the meeting was useful. People speak to be seen, to repeat a point, to fill a silence. Noise has a lot of causes. Usefulness is one of them. The status update has the same shape. If the project is on track, the update sounds confident. The update sounded confident, so the project is on track. Confidence is a tone, and a tone is cheap. It is very convincing to a manager who wanted good news before the slide came up.

If a teammate is sharp, they answer fast in the group chat. They answered fast, so they are sharp. Speed can be the person who was already sitting at the laptop, or the person who replies before reading. The slow reply may be the one who went and checked. Fast replies are the result. Sharpness is a story added afterwards.

If they respected you, they would agree in the meeting. They agreed, so they respect you. Agreement is also fear, fatigue, a wish to leave the room, or a point that was actually fine. The nod is what you saw. Respect is the flattering cause, which is why it sticks.

Politics

This part stays off parties and personalities. The flip is happy to work for every side, and it does not need a name attached.

If a policy worked, some number moved the way people hoped. The number moved, so the policy worked.

Numbers move because of weather, a festival, a different rule, a change in how the number is counted, people changing habits for reasons that had nothing to do with the announcement. Whoever is in office will point at the number. Whoever is out of office will point at a different number and run the same trick. The movement is the result. The policy is one candidate for the cause, and it has to survive the other candidates. A degree in economics does not stop a person from skipping that step when the number flatters them.

If a politician is corrupt, they own expensive things. They own expensive things, so they are corrupt. Expensive things also come from a family, a spouse's work, a loan, a business that existed before the election. A watch you can see is not a case file. A cheap watch is not proof of honesty either. That second jump is the cousin of this mistake, and people use it when they already like the person. Both versions show up between lawyers, between journalists, and at family dinners where everyone thinks they are only being practical.

If voters wanted that particular law, they voted for the people who proposed it. They voted for those people, so they wanted that law. People vote for a local face, a habit, a community, a job, a fear, one other issue that mattered more that year. Reading a win as signed approval of every item on the list is the flip. The winners say it. Commentators say it. It does not get more true because the person saying it has read a lot of politics.

Why trained people still do it

The two sentences share their words. Rain, then wet ground. Wet ground, then rain. If the first one sounded sensible, the ear lets the second one through. It feels like you are repeating yourself. You are not. You turned the arrow around.

It is also fast. Asking what else could cause B needs a list. B happened, so A, is already finished. You get a verdict without the wait, and a verdict feels like thinking.

And it protects a story you already liked. The distant partner is cheating. The promoted colleague is brilliant. The polite child proves the parenting. The number on the screen proves your side. School adds vocabulary. It does not, on its own, force the list of other causes. I think this is why the same week can hold a lawyer reading nerves as guilt, a doctor reading one lab value as a diagnosis, a journalist reading a hole in an account as a lie, and a manager reading a confident slide as a healthy project.

If the person did it, they will look nervous. They look nervous, so they did it.

That is the courtroom version, and it is worth writing out because a courtroom is where people are sure they are being logical. A courtroom is also a frightening room. Innocent people sweat. So do guilty people. So does anyone who slept badly and then got accused in public. Nervousness is the result you can see from the bench. Guilt is one cause of it. Being on trial is another.

None of this needs a study behind it. You only have to listen to the next conversation you are already in, and separate the sentence that was actually earned from the sentence that got in by reversing it.

What to do with it

When a conclusion arrives in the shape "B, therefore A", say both sentences out loud, in ordinary words.

Rain makes the ground wet. Wet ground means it rained.

The second one has to stand on its own, and a lot of the time it cannot. That is useful. Very often the second one is the sentence people are actually using. They think they are still using the first.

Then ask what else produces B. Two other causes are enough to stop the automatic jump. If those causes are still possible, you do not have A yet. You have B, and a guess.

This will slow you down in an argument. People want the flip, because the flip is a verdict. You are offering a pause instead. I still think the pause is worth it. A lot of bad calls, about money, about what another person feels, about whether someone's work is actually good, start from a sentence that was only ever true in the other direction.
